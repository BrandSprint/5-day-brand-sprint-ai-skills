# Sprint · Day 2 · Positioning & Essence

Load `sprint-board` first; its rule, project folder, report format and placement rules apply in full. Open by reading `00 Project/project.md`, `00 Project/decisions.md` and the latest report in `02 The Sprint/Day 1/`, including what the room decided in the Day 2 morning check.

## When

The evening of Day 2, after the room locked the Brand Strategic Direction and the Brand Positioning Statement. If either is empty or still reads "Write here", stop after the inventory and say the day has not locked.

## What this skill is for

Day 2 turns the insight into a direction in three moves: vision and purpose checked against the insight, the essence found and locked with the purpose, the idea placed in the market with three statements. The guide's own line for a sound direction: insight, purpose and essence are three lengths of one decision. This skill checks whether they are, and whether every clause of the position can be pointed at on the walls. It does not write any of them, not as a suggestion, not as an example of a better one.

## Frames to read

1. `Final Strategic Insights` (the lock from Day 1; read it as written and carry it through every test)
2. `Vision & Purpose Revisited`, and `Vision & Purpose` from Discovery for the before-and-after
3. `Essence Map`
4. `Brand Strategic Direction`
5. `Simplified Value Statement`, `Simplified Distinctiveness Statement`, `Brand Positioning Statement`
6. `Optional - Distinctiveness Matrix`, if filled
7. The four Evidence Walls, for pointing
8. `Optional - Main Customer Profile` and `Optional - Main Competitor Profile`, if filled
9. Any `Facilitator checks · …` frame

Report the inventory first.

## Canvas layouts

Vision & Purpose Revisited: Vision in the left third (x under 0.35), a box; Purpose in the right two thirds, a flower-shaped area with the shared purpose at its centre.

Essence Map: candidates braindumped on the left (x under 0.40). Four filter boxes stacked top to bottom in the middle (x 0.40 to 0.60): Relevance, Differentiation, Sustainability, Authenticity. Survivors on the right (x over 0.65), a small area; the guide says stop at three.

Brand Strategic Direction: three stacked fields. Top (y under 0.35) the strategic insight, copied word for word. Middle (y 0.35 to 0.65) the purpose, one sentence starting "We exist to". Bottom (y over 0.65) the final essence, one to three words, with its case underneath.

Simplified Value Statement: a formula laid out top to bottom: brand name, "helps", target audience, "to", main benefit. The final sentence in the box at the bottom (y over 0.75).

Simplified Distinctiveness Statement: same shape: brand name, "is the only / best", category, "that", distinctiveness. Final sentence in the bottom box.

Brand Positioning Statement: brand name, "is the only / the best", category (top row); "to", target audience, what we do (second row); how we do it, main strengths (third row); why we do it, brand purpose (fourth row); optional tagline in the bottom box.

Distinctiveness Matrix: left to right. Your Strengths, Competitor Strengths, Customers Key Needs (three input columns, x under 0.45). Then the sorting field: We are the same (grey), Uniqueness (green, top), Betterness (green, top right), Only they have (red, bottom), They are better (red, bottom right). Then the numbered Distinctiveness list on the far right (x over 0.88).

## Tests, in order

### T1 · Vision and purpose against the insight
Compare the Discovery version with the revisited version. What changed, quoted. Is there a sentence on what moved and why (the guide asks for it)? Purpose form: one sentence, starts with "We exist to", has a verb and a beneficiary. Vision form: where the brand wants to be or what it wants to change, not a why. A purpose written as a vision or the reverse is a finding. Does the purpose answer the locked insight? Name the clause of the insight it answers, or "does not address the insight".

### T2 · The essence candidates and the filters
Count survivors on the Essence Map; more than three is a finding (the filters were too soft, in the guide's words). For each survivor: one to three words, no verb, not a tagline, not a category word. For each filter, what on the walls supports the pass: Relevance (Audience wall), Differentiation (Competitors wall, and which competitor already claims the same), Sustainability (Organization & Brand wall), Authenticity (Organization & Brand wall, and the values the room wrote on Day 3 if the run happens later). A survivor with no evidence on a filter: "passed in the room without evidence on [filter]".

### T3 · The lock: three lengths of one decision
The Brand Strategic Direction frame. Is the insight copied word for word from Day 1, or did it change at the copy (quote the difference)? Purpose and essence: do they say the same thing at two lengths? Name the shared idea, or name the gap (usually the beneficiary or the verb). The case under the essence: does it say what it means in practice and how it clears the four filters? The guide: if the purpose and the essence say the same thing at two lengths, that is alignment, and there is nothing to fix. Say so when it holds.

### T4 · The value statement
Formula followed: brand, "helps", best-fit customers, "to", main benefit. The audience: is it a group that can be found and reached, or "everyone who"? The benefit: is it a benefit (about the customer) or a feature (about the product)? Apply the guide's "why does that matter" until the answer is about the customer; if the written benefit is still a feature, finding. Does the main benefit match what customers said matters most on the Audience wall? Quote the sticky, or "no evidence found on the walls".

### T5 · The distinctiveness statement
Formula followed: brand, "only" or "best", category, "that", distinctiveness. "Only" needs proof that no competitor does it: search the Competitors wall; a competitor sticky that claims the same breaks "only" (quote it). "Best" needs criteria: are they written? The category: narrow or broad, and did the room choose it ("phone maker" and "technology company" are different fights)? Is the distinctiveness at the meeting point of what the brand does well, what customers need, and what rivals can't copy? Point at one sticky for each, or name the missing one. If the Distinctiveness Matrix was run: is the chosen distinctiveness on its numbered list, and does its line answer a customer need?

### T6 · The positioning statement
Copied, not rewritten: each field should match its source (category and strengths from the distinctiveness statement, customers and value from the value statement, purpose from the direction). Quote every field that differs from its source; a rewritten field is a finding because the guide says to fix the piece it came from, not the assembly. Read as one sentence: does it hold together? "Only" and "best" carry over with their proof. The "because" is the purpose, as written on the direction board. The competitor signature test: could the closest competitor sign this sentence? Name the competitor sticky that makes it possible, or "no".

### T7 · Ancestry
Every clause of the positioning statement traced back: to the insight (the truth about people), to a wall (the proof), or to nothing. The clauses with nothing behind them are findings; the guide's rule is that orphans go out.

### T8 · Optional profiles
If the Main Customer Profile is filled: does the audience in the value statement match the main group the room chose? If the Main Competitor Profile is filled: does the distinctiveness sit on the open flank the room circled?

## Output

Write the report in the `sprint-board` format to `02 The Sprint/Day 2/report-<date>.md`. In Readings: the three statements with each clause traced to its source board and wall, and the one-line "three lengths" check (insight, purpose, essence side by side, with the shared idea named or the gap named). Opinion, short, labelled. Next steps for the Day 3 morning check.

Closing question, exactly: "Place the top three on the board as black CHECK stickies in a frame 'Facilitator checks · Day 2'? yes / no / choose". Follow `sprint-board` for placement. Append the run to `decisions.md`.

## What this skill never does

Write a purpose, an essence, a category, a benefit, a distinctiveness or a positioning statement, in any form. Choose between essence candidates. Mark a filter as passed on its own judgement. Supply a competitor fact that is not on the Competitors wall. Write to the board before the closing question is answered.
