# Sprint · Day 3 · Nature & Identity

Load `sprint-board` first; its rule, project folder, report format and placement rules apply in full. Open by reading `00 Project/project.md`, `00 Project/decisions.md` and the latest report in `02 The Sprint/Day 2/`, including what the room decided in the Day 3 morning check.

## When

The evening of Day 3, after the room locked the values, the archetype, the sliders, the tone and the persona. If the values list or the archetype is empty, stop after the inventory and say the day has not locked.

## What this skill is for

Day 3 defines the brand as a person, and the guide's test for every piece of it is upstream: a value has to change a decision, an archetype has to follow from purpose and values, a slider position has to match the archetype, a tone has to be one the closest competitor could not use, and a persona trait with nothing upstream goes. Playology's own check is the model: "authority" survived only as unobtrusive authority, the kind that gathers a family rather than commands it. This skill runs those tests slowly and draws the coherence map the room can see in the morning.

## Frames to read

1. `Brand Strategic Direction` and `Brand Positioning Statement` (the Day 2 locks)
2. `Core Brand Values`
3. `Rewrite Brand Values`
4. `Brand Archetypes`
5. `Look & Feel Slider`
6. `Tone of voice`
7. `Brand as a Person` (not on the template yet; if missing, say so and ask the facilitator whether the persona exists elsewhere)
8. `Evidence Wall - Organization & Brand` and `Evidence Wall - Competitors`, for pointing
9. Any `Facilitator checks · …` frame

Report the inventory first.

## Canvas layouts

Core Brand Values: top area (y under 0.45) "Write and cluster your brand values": stickies, clustered. Middle (y 0.45 to 0.70) "Prioritize your values": the kept three to six. Bottom rows (y over 0.70): the template's value chips (Freedom, Integrity, Innovation, and so on), which are the template's own and not the room's unless a chip was moved up.

Rewrite Brand Values: six rows, Brand value 1 to 6 (y from 0.15 to 0.80, evenly spaced), each with the value and the line on what it makes the brand do and stop doing. The template's examples at the bottom (Customer-centricity, Deep expertise, and so on) are the template's own.

Brand Archetypes: four columns by family, left to right: Independence, Stability, Belonging, Mastery. Three archetypes per column, top to bottom. Independence: Innocent, Sage, Explorer. Stability: Outlaw, Hero, Magician. Belonging: Everyman, Lover, Jester. Mastery: Caregiver, Creator, Ruler. The room's choice is a sticky, mark or dot on a card; a second mark is the secondary.

Look & Feel Slider: eleven pairs, top to bottom: Feminine/Masculine, Simple/Detailed, Young/Established, Subtle/Bold, Traditional/Modern, Friendly/Authoritative, Playful/Corporate, Disruptive/Mainstream, Industrial/Natural, Economical/Luxury, Less Colorful/More Colorful. Scale from -3 (left word) to +3 (right word), zero in the middle (x about 0.52). A slider's position is the dot or sticky the room placed; individual marks before the group agreed may remain, so the agreed position is the one on the centre line of the row or the one marked as agreed.

Tone of voice: four horizontal scales, top to bottom: Humour (Funny to Serious), Respect (Irreverent to Respectful), Enthusiasm (Enthusiastic to Matter-of-fact), Confidence (Assertive to Humble). Position by the dot's x. Custom Attributes box at the bottom (y over 0.80) with stickies. The in-tone and off-tone examples are stickies, usually beside the box.

Brand as a Person: four question columns across the top (Appearance & presence, Behavior & role, Relationship to you, Personality), a Name & photo box top right (x over 0.85), and the open area below for the persona.

## Tests, in order

### T1 · Values, three to six, each doing work
Count the prioritised values: under three or over six is a finding. For each value: a behaviour line on Core Brand Values, and a "what it makes the brand do / stop doing" line on Rewrite Brand Values; missing either is a finding. The decision test: which decision this year would be different because of this value? If the room wrote one, quote it; if not, finding, severity weakens. The any-brand-would-sign test: a value every competitor would also claim (quality, innovation, customer focus as bare words) is a finding unless the behaviour line makes it specific. Values against the Organization & Brand wall: which sticky shows the company already lives it; "no evidence found on the walls" otherwise.

### T2 · The archetype
Primary chosen, and its family. Does the family match the purpose's motivation (Independence, Stability, Belonging, Mastery)? Does the primary follow from the values: list each value and whether the archetype would hold it. A Ruler with Caregiver values, in the guide's example, is a finding. The test the guide asks the room to run: how would this character answer a complaint, or say no? If the room wrote it, quote; if not, reading. Secondary: present only if the primary leaves something out; name what it covers or flag it as decoration.

### T3 · The sliders
All eleven agreed? Unagreed rows are findings. A brand at zero on every scale, or on more than seven, is a finding (nothing that makes it its own). The three or four furthest from zero: are they pulled out as the brief? Each slider position against the archetype: a Caregiver on Authoritative and Corporate, a Jester on Serious and Corporate, an Outlaw on Mainstream, a Ruler on Playful, and so on; name each tension. Sliders against the values: a value of Simplicity with Detailed at +3, for instance.

### T4 · Tone of voice
Four positions set, custom attributes read. Examples: one in tone and one off tone must exist; missing is a finding, severity blocks, because the guide says copywriters work from those. The competitor test: could the closest competitor on the Competitors wall say the in-tone line? Answer with the competitor sticky or "no". The hard-case test: did the room write the tone in an apology, a price increase, a bad review? If not, reading. Tone against archetype and sliders: a Funny tone with a Serious slider position, an Assertive tone with a Humble value.

### T5 · The persona
Name, face, voice present. Every trait traced upstream to a value, the archetype, a slider or a tone position; traits with nothing upstream are findings (the guide: a trait with nothing upstream goes). The admiration trap: a persona described as what the room admires rather than what the brand is; name the traits that look like admiration. The well-known-person comparison: does it match the archetype?

### T6 · Ancestry to the direction
Everything above against the Day 2 locks: does the character answer the insight, serve the purpose, carry the essence? Name one element of Day 3 that contradicts a Day 2 lock, if any, quoting both.

## Output

Write the report in the `sprint-board` format to `02 The Sprint/Day 3/report-<date>.md`. In Readings, the coherence map: one line per element (insight, purpose, essence, each value, archetype primary and secondary, each slider far from zero, each tone position, the persona's main traits), with the element it rests on and a mark where the link is weak or missing. Opinion, short, labelled. Next steps for the Day 4 morning check.

Closing question, exactly: "Place the top three on the board as black CHECK stickies in a frame 'Facilitator checks · Day 3'? yes / no / choose". Follow `sprint-board` for placement. Append the run to `decisions.md`.

## What this skill never does

Write a value, a behaviour line, an archetype choice, a slider position, a tone example or a persona trait. Rank the room's values. Decide the archetype. Supply a competitor's tone from outside the Competitors wall. Write to the board before the closing question is answered.
